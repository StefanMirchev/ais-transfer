How it works

Everything revolves around one pair: a Seller and a Purchaser.

You keep a list of the businesses on the other side of your invoices, and AIS uses that list to know who should receive what — and where to bring in what's been sent to you.

1

Prepare

You save an invoice for a Purchaser the way you always have. Nothing changes about how the invoice itself gets made.

2

Upload

Choose Upload Invoices from the Main Menu. AIS gets everything ready and sends each invoice to the right Purchaser.

3

Deliver

AIS places the invoice where that Purchaser will look for it, sorted by who it came from — no shared inbox, no sorting by hand.

4

Receive

The Purchaser brings it in with Download Invoices — by hand, or automatically at set times of day — and it's on their computer, ready to file.

Inside AIS

The Main Menu, and what each option does.

The Main Menu is the small window that opens when you start AIS. It stays out of the way in the corner of your screen — every option is one double-click away. Point at any option and press F1 for a reminder of what it does.

1

List Seller

Keep the businesses you send invoices to — names, phone, email, and status at a glance.

2

List Purchaser

The same list, the other direction: the businesses that send invoices to you.

3

Upload Invoices

Sends out anything you've prepared for the Purchasers on your Seller list, and tells you when it's done.

4

Download Invoices

Brings in anything your Sellers have sent you, sorted by who it belongs to.

5

Automatic Download Invoices

Turns hands-off receiving on or off — AIS checks for new invoices at set times, without a click from you.

Messages

Leave a short note for any Seller or Purchaser straight from their list. A small red marker lets you know when one is waiting to be read.

Checking your space

Check Loaded shows the Load Limit, Consumption, and room still free for any Seller — so a full mailbox never comes as a surprise.

Files & IDs

Four kinds of files. Nothing else gets through.

You don't need to remember any of this to use AIS — but if you ever see one of these names on disk, here's what it is. Anything that doesn't match is treated as clutter and removed, on your computer and wherever else it was found.

What it looks likeWhat it is
1234567890.pdf
10 digits + .pdf		 An invoice.
1234567890.txt
10 digits + .txt		 The item details for that same invoice, when there are any.
12345678.txt
8 digits + .txt		 A list of invoices.
message.txt A message between a Seller and a Purchaser.

Every Seller is given a set amount of space to send invoices in. Check Loaded breaks it down into three numbers:

LOAD LIMIT — total space given CONSUMPTION — space already used NOT LOADED — room still free

About

Built for the part of the job nobody enjoys.

Sending and chasing invoices eats up time that has nothing to do with actually running a business. AIS exists to take that one job off your desk: it carries invoices between the people who send them and the people who need them, on a schedule, without anyone opening an email client.

It's built to sit quietly in the corner of a screen. There's no dashboard to learn, no login to remember — just a short list of who you do business with, and a handful of options that do exactly what they say.

Questions

Before you get started

Do I need to be a Seller or a Purchaser to use AIS?

Either, or both. If you send invoices, you'll keep a Seller list. If you receive them, you'll keep a Purchaser list. Many businesses do both at once.

What happens if a Seller's space runs out?

AIS tells you exactly which invoices couldn't be sent and by how much the space was exceeded, so you can free up room or ask for more.

Can invoices get lost or misfiled?

AIS only recognizes four file patterns and sorts everything by the Seller and Purchaser it belongs to. Anything that doesn't match those patterns is removed rather than left to cause confusion later.

Do I have to check for new invoices myself?

No — turn on Automatic Download Invoices and AIS checks for you at set times each day. You can switch it off again just as easily.

Where do received invoices end up?

On your own computer, sorted by who sent them. AIS doesn't delete anything you've received — it just reminds you to move them into your own archive from time to time.
